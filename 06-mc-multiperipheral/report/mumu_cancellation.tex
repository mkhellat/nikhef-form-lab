\title{Reproducing Vermaseren's numerical-instability claim for\\
$\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$}
\author{nikhef-form-lab, Part 5 (\texttt{06-mc-multiperipheral})}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
J.A.M.\ Vermaseren's \emph{Two Photon Processes at Very High Energies}
(NIKHEF-H/82-15, 1982) states, discussing the reaction
$e^+e^-\to e^+e^-\mu^+\mu^-$ at PETRA/LEP energies: ``If the matrix element
were to be completely evaluated in the standard fashion -- i.e.\ expressing
it completely in terms of 4-vector products and then substituting their
numerical values for each Monte Carlo generated point in phase space --
then the cancellations between the various terms would be so bad that even
the 60 bit accuracy of a CDC computer would not suffice.'' This report
reproduces that claim end to end for the two-photon subprocess
$\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$: deriving the squared matrix element by hand
via the naive (``standard fashion'') route and via Vermaseren's
Schouten-identity-stabilized route, cross-checking both derivations
symbolically, and then evaluating both forms numerically in IEEE double
precision at kinematic points approaching the multiperipheral peak
($t_1,t_2\to0$) to measure directly how many significant digits are lost
in the naive route.
\end{abstract}

\section{The process and the two diagrams}
\label{sec:diagrams}

We study the subprocess $\gamma^*(q_1)\,\gamma^*(q_2)\to\mu^-(p_6)\,\mu^+(p_7)$,
following Vermaseren's Fig.~3 labeling (reproduced in
Figures~\ref{fig:diagram-k1}--\ref{fig:diagram-k2}). This is the
matrix-element core of the full $e^+e^-\to e^+e^-\mu^+\mu^-$ two-photon
process (Fig.~1 of the same paper): the electron and positron each radiate
a virtual photon ($q_1$, $q_2$), which then fuse via muon exchange into the
$\mu^+\mu^-$ pair. The electron/positron lines and the two photon
propagators $1/q_1^2,\,1/q_2^2$ are common to both diagrams and are not the
source of the numerical instability discussed here; the instability lives
entirely in the $\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$ subprocess itself, so we
isolate it.

There are exactly two tree diagrams, differing by which photon attaches to
the muon line first:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diagram $k_1$}: photon $q_1$ attaches first; the internal
    muon propagator carries momentum $k_1 = p_6-q_1 = q_2-p_7$.
  \item \textbf{Diagram $k_2$} (crossed): photon $q_2$ attaches first;
    the internal muon propagator carries momentum $k_2 = p_6-q_2 = q_1-p_7$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Kinematic conventions}

On-shell conditions: $p_6^2=p_7^2=m^2$ (muon mass), while the photons are
virtual with $q_1^2=t_1$, $q_2^2=t_2$ (generally $t_1,t_2\neq0$, since these
are internal propagators of the full process, not real external photons).
Momentum conservation at the $\gamma^*\gamma^*$ vertex reads
$q_1+q_2=p_6+p_7\equiv Q$. We will also use $k_1^2=(p_6-q_1)^2$ and
$k_2^2=(p_6-q_2)^2$, with propagator denominators
$D_1\equiv k_1^2-m^2$, $D_2\equiv k_2^2-m^2$.

The ``multiperipheral peak'' regime the paper's warning is about is
$t_1,t_2\to0$ (both photons nearly on-shell/real) at fixed, large $Q^2=s$
-- the same corner of phase space studied numerically for the $\pi^0$ case
in Topic 5.

\section{Feynman rules and the amplitude}
\label{sec:feynman-rules}

Using standard QED Feynman rules (photon-fermion vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$,
fermion propagator $i(\slashed k+m)/(k^2-m^2)$):

\begin{align}
  \mathcal M_1 &= (-ie)^2\,\bar u(p_6)\,(-i\slashed e_1)\,
    \frac{i(\slashed k_1+m)}{D_1}\,(-i\slashed e_2)\,v(p_7)
    \label{eq:M1} \\
  \mathcal M_2 &= (-ie)^2\,\bar u(p_6)\,(-i\slashed e_2)\,
    \frac{i(\slashed k_2+m)}{D_2}\,(-i\slashed e_1)\,v(p_7)
    \label{eq:M2}
\end{align}

Dropping the overall coupling and bookkeeping factors of $i$ into a common
prefactor (matching Vermaseren's own convention, cf.\ formula (III.3) of
\texttt{twophoton.pdf}), the total amplitude is

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal M \;\propto\; \bar u(p_6)\,N\,v(p_7), \qquad
  N \equiv \slashed e_1\,\frac{\slashed k_1+m}{D_1}\,\slashed e_2
    \;+\; \slashed e_2\,\frac{\slashed k_2+m}{D_2}\,\slashed e_1
  \label{eq:N-def}
\end{equation}

This matches the left-hand side of formula (III.3) in \texttt{twophoton.pdf}
exactly, confirming the starting point before proceeding to the trace
algebra.

\section{The naive (``standard fashion'') squared amplitude}
\label{sec:naive}

This section carries out the calculation the paper warns about: expand
$N$ into pieces, form the spin-summed squared amplitude as a sum of Dirac
traces, reduce every trace to products of 4-vector dots, and keep the
propagator denominators $D_1$ and $D_2$ as they stand. No Schouten
identity and no Levi-Civita rearrangement is used. The algebra is
implemented in \texttt{scripts/mumu\_trace\_lib.py} and driven by
\texttt{scripts/mumu\_naive\_trace.py}; the checks that can falsify it are
listed in Section~\ref{sec:naive-checks}. Full term listings and the
subtleties that showed up while deriving them are in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-naive}.

\subsection{Split of $N$ into four pieces}
\label{sec:N-split}

Write each diagram's numerator as a massless slash piece plus a mass
piece:
\begin{align}
  N_1 &= \frac{\slashed e_1\,\slashed k_1\,\slashed e_2}{D_1}, &
  N_2 &= \frac{m\,\slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2}{D_1}, \nonumber\\
  N_3 &= \frac{\slashed e_2\,\slashed k_2\,\slashed e_1}{D_2}, &
  N_4 &= \frac{m\,\slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1}{D_2},
  \label{eq:N-pieces}
\end{align}
so $N=N_1+N_2+N_3+N_4$. For real polarization vectors the conjugate
amplitude $\bar N$ is obtained by reversing the order of the gamma
matrices in each piece (e.g.\ $\overline{N_1}=\slashed e_2\,\slashed k_1\,
\slashed e_1/D_1$). This is an assumption of the calculation; it is the
standard real-$e$ convention, not something we re-derived from a complex
polarization basis.

\subsection{Spin sum as a $4\times 4$ of traces}
\label{sec:16-terms}

The spin-summed squared amplitude (up to the overall coupling stripped in
Section~\ref{sec:feynman-rules}) is
\begin{equation}
  S = \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[(\slashed p_6+m)\,N\,(\slashed p_7-m)\,\bar N\right]
    = \sum_{i,j=1}^{4} T_{ij},
  \qquad
  T_{ij} =
  \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[(\slashed p_6+m)\,N_i\,(\slashed p_7-m)\,\overline{N_j}\right].
  \label{eq:S-def}
\end{equation}
Each $T_{ij}$ still contains the factor $1/(D_a D_b)$ from the two pieces
involved. Expanding $(\slashed p_6+m)$ and $(\slashed p_7-m)$ gives four
ordinary gamma-chain traces per $T_{ij}$. A chain with an odd number of
gamma matrices has vanishing trace; that kills some of the four
contributions outright (see the worked example below).

Reduction of an even-length chain uses the standard recursive contraction
(with $\mathrm{Tr}[1]=4$)
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Tr}[\gamma^{v_1}\cdots\gamma^{v_n}]
  = \sum_{k=2}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\,(v_1{\cdot}v_k)\,
    \mathrm{Tr}[\text{chain without $v_1,v_k$}].
  \label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
There is no $\gamma^5$ in this QED amplitude, so no Levi-Civita tensor
appears. That is by construction of the Feynman rules used in
Section~\ref{sec:feynman-rules}, not an extra truncation.

\subsection{Worked example: $T_{22}$}
\label{sec:T22}

Take $i=j=2$, the shortest nontrivial diagonal piece:
\begin{equation}
  T_{22}
  = \frac{m^2}{D_1^2}\,
    \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[
      (\slashed p_6+m)\,
      \slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2\,
      (\slashed p_7-m)\,
      \slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1
    \right].
\end{equation}
The four sub-traces are:
\begin{align}
  A &= \mathrm{Tr}[\slashed p_6\,\slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2\,
                   \slashed p_7\,\slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1],
  \nonumber\\
  B &= (-m)\,\mathrm{Tr}[\slashed p_6\,\slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2\,
                         \slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1],
  \nonumber\\
  C &= m\,\mathrm{Tr}[\slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2\,
                      \slashed p_7\,\slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1],
  \nonumber\\
  D &= (-m^2)\,\mathrm{Tr}[\slashed e_1\,\slashed e_2\,
                           \slashed e_2\,\slashed e_1].
  \label{eq:T22-parts}
\end{align}
$B$ and $C$ each have five gamma matrices, so $B=C=0$. The four-gamma
identity
$\mathrm{Tr}[\slashed a\,\slashed b\,\slashed c\,\slashed d]
=4\bigl[(a{\cdot}b)(c{\cdot}d)-(a{\cdot}c)(b{\cdot}d)+(a{\cdot}d)(b{\cdot}c)\bigr]$
gives
\begin{equation}
  D = (-m^2)\cdot 4\,(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}e_2).
\end{equation}
The six-gamma trace $A$ is reduced by~\eqref{eq:contraction}; after
imposing the on-shell conditions $p_6{\cdot}p_6=p_7{\cdot}p_7=m^2$
(Section~\ref{sec:naive-assumptions}) one obtains
\begin{align}
  A &= 4(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}e_2)(p_6{\cdot}p_7)
      - 8(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}p_6)(e_2{\cdot}p_7)
      \nonumber\\
    &\quad + 16(e_1{\cdot}e_2)(e_1{\cdot}p_6)(e_2{\cdot}p_7)
      - 8(e_1{\cdot}p_6)(e_1{\cdot}p_7)(e_2{\cdot}e_2).
\end{align}
Putting $A$ and $D$ together with the prefactor $m^2/D_1^2$,
\begin{align}
  T_{22}
  &= \frac{1}{D_1^2}\Bigl[
       -4(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}e_2)\,m^4
       +4(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}e_2)\,m^2(p_6{\cdot}p_7)
       \nonumber\\
  &\hspace{2.2em}
       -8(e_1{\cdot}e_1)(e_2{\cdot}p_6)(e_2{\cdot}p_7)\,m^2
       +16(e_1{\cdot}e_2)(e_1{\cdot}p_6)(e_2{\cdot}p_7)\,m^2
       \nonumber\\
  &\hspace{2.2em}
       -8(e_1{\cdot}p_6)(e_1{\cdot}p_7)(e_2{\cdot}e_2)\,m^2
     \Bigr].
  \label{eq:T22-result}
\end{align}
This matches the symbolic output of \texttt{mumu\_naive\_trace.py} for
\texttt{T[N2,N2]} exactly (term by term). The same script evaluates the
other fifteen $T_{ij}$; those expressions are not re-derived by hand in
the main text.

\subsection{Result of the full sum}
\label{sec:naive-result}

Running \texttt{scripts/mumu\_naive\_trace.py} (which uses the shared
library above) yields:
\begin{itemize}
  \item none of the sixteen $T_{ij}$ vanishes as a whole;
  \item after expanding and collecting, the sum $S$ contains
    \textbf{98} distinct monomials in external dot products;
  \item those monomials split by denominator as
    $18$ with $1/D_1^2$, $18$ with $1/D_2^2$, and $62$ with $1/(D_1 D_2)$.
\end{itemize}
The separate $D_1$, $D_2$, and $D_1 D_2$ families are the concrete form of
the paper's ``standard fashion'' expression: there is no single common
denominator at this stage, and large cancellations between families (and
within a family) are left for floating-point evaluation to discover. The
machine-readable listing is
\texttt{scripts/generated/mumu\_naive\_Tij.txt}, reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:term-dump}.

\subsection{Assumptions, and what falsifies them}
\label{sec:naive-assumptions}

The following are imposed or not imposed, deliberately:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Imposed:} $p_6{\cdot}p_6=p_7{\cdot}p_7=m^2$.
    These are the physical muon mass shells. If they are left as free
    symbols, the squared Ward identity of
    Section~\ref{sec:naive-checks} fails (a nonzero remainder of order
    $10^2$ monomials). So the on-shell conditions are not optional bookkeeping.
  \item \textbf{Not imposed in $S$:} momentum conservation relating
    $\{e_1,e_2,k_1,k_2,p_6,p_7\}$, and the definitions
    $D_i=k_i^2-m^2$. In the naive sum, all those dots and both $D_i$ are
    independent symbols. That is the literal ``express in 4-vector
    products'' expansion; further kinematic reduction is a separate step
    and is \emph{not} claimed here.
  \item \textbf{Assumed:} real $e_1,e_2$ so that $\bar N$ is the gamma-order
    reverse of $N$. Not cross-checked against a complex-polarization
    formulation in this repository.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Checks that can fail}
\label{sec:naive-checks}

Two independent Ward-identity scripts test gauge invariance of the same
$N$ that enters $S$:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{scripts/mumu\_gauge\_check.py} substitutes $e_1\to q_1$ in
    $N$ alone, reduces with the on-shell Dirac equations
    $\bar u(p_6)\slashed p_6=m\,\bar u(p_6)$ and
    $\slashed p_7\,v(p_7)=-m\,v(p_7)$, and substitutes $D_i=k_i^2-m^2$.
    Result: diagram~1 alone $\to-\slashed e_2$, diagram~2 alone
    $\to+\slashed e_2$, sum $=0$. Neither diagram is gauge invariant by
    itself; only the sum is. (An earlier draft of this script had a
    duplicated $k_1$ token in one word list; the hand reduction disagreed
    with the machine until that bug was fixed.)
  \item \texttt{scripts/mumu\_gauge\_check\_squared.py} substitutes
    $e_1\to q_1$ \emph{only} on the $N$ side of~\eqref{eq:S-def}, leaves
    $\bar N$ physical, and uses the same trace code as the naive sum.
    Result: the full expression vanishes. Replacing $e_1$ on both $N$ and
    $\bar N$ would give the trivial $0\cdot 0=0$ and is not used.
\end{enumerate}
Both scripts exit with a clear failure message if the identity does not
hold. As of this writing both pass. Index and run order:
\texttt{scripts/README-mumu.md}.

\section{Vermaseren's stable form}
\label{sec:stable}

Section~\ref{sec:naive} expanded $N$ into 4-vector products and left
$D_1$ and $D_2$ as separate families. The paper's remedy
(\texttt{twophoton.pdf}, Section~III and Appendix~C) is to rewrite the
\emph{sum} of the two diagrams so that gauge cancellations sit inside
Levi-Civita tensors and a single common propagator denominator, then to
square that form and reduce products of $\varepsilon$-tensors with the
Schouten identity. The load-bearing structural claims are recorded here;
the full Appendix~C reduction chain for the cross pieces $M_{12}$,
$M_{21}$ is not re-derived term-by-term in this section (the paper itself
calls that algebra ``far from trivial'' and checked it with Schoonschip).
A machine identity between Section~\ref{sec:naive} and this section is
left to Section~\ref{sec:symbolic-check}.

\subsection{Common-denominator, gauge-safe amplitude (III.3)}
\label{sec:stable-amp}

The starting point is the same $N$ as in~\eqref{eq:N-def}. Using the
on-shell Dirac equations
$\bar u(p_6)(\slashed p_6-m)=0$ and $(\slashed p_7+m)v(p_7)=0$,
gamma-matrix algebra, and the Schouten identity in four dimensions, the
paper rewrites the \emph{sum} of the two diagrams as a single structure
with denominator $D_1 D_2$ (formula~(III.3)). Schematically,
\begin{equation}
  \bar u(p_6)\,N\,v(p_7)
  = -\varepsilon^{q_1 e_1\alpha\mu}\,
      \varepsilon_{q_1 e_1\beta\mu}\,
      \frac{\bar u(p_6)\,
        \bigl(\gamma^\beta\slashed p_6\gamma_\alpha
              +\chi\,\gamma_\alpha\slashed p_7\gamma^\beta\bigr)
      \,v(p_7)}{D_1 D_2},
  \label{eq:III3-schematic}
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ encodes the relative weight of the two spinor orderings in
the paper's notation, and the precise index routing matches
\texttt{twophoton.pdf}. The point that matters for numerics is
structural:
\begin{itemize}
  \item neither diagram alone is written in a form that vanishes under
    $e_1\to q_1$; the Levi-Civita factors make the
    \emph{sum} gauge-safe by construction (consistent with the Ward
    checks of Section~\ref{sec:naive-checks});
  \item $D_1$ and $D_2$ appear only as the product $D_1 D_2$, not as
    separate $1/D_1^2$, $1/D_2^2$, and $1/(D_1 D_2)$ families.
\end{itemize}
Under~(III.5) the paper states explicitly that remaining cancellations
inside the Levi-Civita tensors are due to spin structure and are ``not
serious'' compared with the naive 4-vector-product cancellations.

\subsection{Squared form and the $M_{ij}$ decomposition (III.10)}
\label{sec:stable-Mij}

After squaring~(III.3)/(III.4), tracing over muon spins, and attaching
the electron/positron (or proton) currents in the two-photon
factorization, the squared matrix element for two spin-$\tfrac12$ point
fermions is cast as (formula~(III.10))
\begin{equation}
  |\mathcal M|^2
  = \frac{
      W_1^{(1)}W_1^{(2)} M_{11}
      + W_1^{(1)}W_2^{(2)} M_{12}
      + W_2^{(1)}W_1^{(2)} M_{21}
      + W_2^{(1)}W_2^{(2)} M_{22}
    }{
      \Bigl(
        q_1^2\, q_2^2\,
        \bigl[(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2 - (Q{\cdot}q_1)^2\bigr]
      \Bigr)^2
    },
  \label{eq:III10}
\end{equation}
with $Q\equiv q_1+q_2$. The weights $W_1,W_2$ are photon-vertex structure
functions; for point particles the paper's normalization gives
$W_1=W_2=1$ (formula~(III.9)), so~\eqref{eq:III10} collapses to
$(M_{11}+M_{12}+M_{21}+M_{22})$ over the displayed denominator.

The four pieces $M_{ij}$ (formula~(III.11)) are polynomials in dot
products and Levi-Civita contractions of $\{q_1,q_2,Q,p_1\}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $M_{11}$ --- no residual $\varepsilon$ tensor (``pure QED'' block);
  \item $M_{12}$, $M_{21}$ --- one $\varepsilon$ structure each (need the
    Appendix~C identities~(C13)/(C14));
  \item $M_{22}$ --- the densest Levi-Civita content.
\end{itemize}
This is the same \emph{recipe} as \texttt{pi0.c}'s split into
\texttt{part1}..\,\texttt{part4} (Gram determinant plus minors), but here
the four blocks are cross terms in the $(W_1,W_2)$ bilinears rather than
minors of one Gram matrix.

\subsection{Denominator: analogous to \texttt{pi0.c}'s \texttt{la}, not identical}
\label{sec:stable-denom}

The factor $(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$ in~\eqref{eq:III10} looks
like the quadratic bracket inside \texttt{pi0.c}'s \texttt{la}. As a pure
vector identity (no on-shell relation),
\begin{equation}
  (q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2 - (Q{\cdot}q_1)^2
  = -\,t_1\,(t_1 + 2\,q_1{\cdot}q_2),
  \qquad t_1=q_1^2,\quad Q=q_1+q_2,
  \label{eq:denom-identity}
\end{equation}
which privileges $q_1$ through $Q{\cdot}q_1$. By contrast,
\texttt{pi0.c}'s bracket $t_1 t_2-(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2$ is symmetric in
$t_1,t_2$. On-shell,
$q_1{\cdot}q_2=(\mu^2-t_1-t_2)/2$, the two brackets become different
polynomials in $t_1,t_2,\mu^2$. This was checked in
\texttt{scripts/muon\_pair\_denominator\_check.py} and is recorded in
Topic~6; an earlier draft that claimed a literal match was wrong.
The two denominators are only \emph{analogous} Gram-type invariants of
double-$t$-channel kinematics.

\subsection{What this section does and does not claim}
\label{sec:stable-claims}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Claimed here:} the stable route replaces separate
    $D_1,D_2$ families by a common $D_1 D_2$ and hides gauge cancellation
    in $\varepsilon$ tensors; the squared point-particle answer has the
    $M_{ij}$ shape~\eqref{eq:III10} with $W_i=1$; the denominator bracket
    satisfies~\eqref{eq:denom-identity} and is not \texttt{pi0.c}'s
    \texttt{la}.
  \item \textbf{Not claimed here:} an independent term-by-term
    re-derivation of Appendix~C through~(C13)/(C14), or a proof that the
    expanded $M_{ij}$ equal the 98-term naive sum of
    Section~\ref{sec:naive}. That identity is the job of
    Section~\ref{sec:symbolic-check}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Symbolic cross-check (FORM)}
\label{sec:symbolic-check}

Section~\ref{sec:naive} used a Python Clifford contraction; this section
reimplements the load-bearing identities in \textbf{FORM}, which is the
right tool for Dirac traces at this scale (C is reserved for numeric
digit-loss work in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}; a Rust CAS would not add
domain leverage here).

\subsection{What is checked}
\label{sec:form-checked}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Squared Ward identity in FORM}
    (\texttt{form/mumu\_ward.frm}).
    Same non-trivial test as Section~\ref{sec:naive-checks}: substitute
    $e_1\to q_1$ only in $N$ (diagram~1: $q_1=p_6-k_1$; diagram~2:
    $q_1=p_7+k_2$), leave $\bar N$ physical, take
    $\mathrm{Tr}[(\slashed p_6+m)N_g(\slashed p_7-m)\bar N]$, clear
    $D_1^2 D_2^2$, set $D_i=k_i^2-m^2$, impose
    $p_7=p_6-k_1-k_2$ and $p_6^2=m^2$. FORM prints
    \texttt{Sg = 0}. This is the gauge premise of the stable route
    (Section~\ref{sec:stable}), now machine-checked in FORM rather than
    only in Python.
  \item \textbf{Naive squared amplitude in FORM}
    (\texttt{form/mumu\_naive.frm}).
    Same $N$/$\bar N$ split as Section~\ref{sec:naive}, evaluated with
    \texttt{Trace4}. Provides an independent FORM baseline for later
    comparison to a transcribed~(III.11).
\end{enumerate}
Run from \texttt{form/}:
\begin{verbatim}
make check    # must print PASS: ... Sg = 0
make naive    # optional inspection of S
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Naive $\equiv$ stable subreaction (3.5)}
\label{sec:naive-eq-35}

The stable Levi-Civita form of the $\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$
\emph{subreaction} (before attaching electron currents) is formula~(3.5)
of Nucl.\ Phys.\ B229 (1983) 347 --- preprint~(III.5). In that formula
$Q=p_6-p_7$ (not $q_1+q_2$; the latter $Q$ appears in~(III.10)/(3.10)).
The bracket $B=(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$ equals $D_1 D_2$ with the
paper's $D_i$ identities under~(3.6).

Script \texttt{scripts/mumu\_stable\_35\_check.py} evaluates~(3.5) and
compares it to the Section~\ref{sec:naive} poly; Appendix~\ref{app:verify}
records the transcribed formula, the $Q=p_6-p_7$ convention, the

\subsection{$M_{ij}$ of (III.11) vs $W_i\otimes W_j$ dressing}
\label{sec:M11-check}

Formula~(III.11)/(3.11) splits $|{\cal M}|^2$ into four blocks
$M_{11}$, $M_{12}$, $M_{21}$, $M_{22}$ weighted by the $W_i$ of~(3.8).
With $B=(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$ and $Q=p_6-p_7$, script
\texttt{scripts/mumu\_mij\_check.py} checks each corner separately and
the full sum:
\begin{equation}
  M_{ij}/B^2
  \;\equiv\;
  S_{\mathrm{naive}}
  \text{ contracted with }
  L_{W_i}(q_1,p_1)\otimes L_{W_j}(q_2,p_2),
\end{equation}
and $\sum_{ij}M_{ij}/B^2$ against $L\otimes L$ ($W_1=W_2=1$).
Here $L_{W_1}(q)=2q^2(g-qq/q^2)$ and $L_{W_2}=8\,r_\mu r_\nu$ with
$r=p-(p{\cdot}q)/q^2\,q$. Electron momenta are massless with
$2p_i{\cdot}q_i=q_i^2$. Transcription details, the three-index $\varepsilon$
sign convention in $M_{12}$/$M_{21}$, and the $M_{22}$ bilinear index
order are in Appendix~\ref{app:verify}.

\end{verbatim}

\section{Numerical demonstration of the cancellation}
\label{sec:numerics}

Section~\ref{sec:symbolic-check} checked the Ward premise symbolically in
FORM. This section shows the \emph{numeric} consequence of evaluating the
naive 98-term 4-vector-product polynomial of Section~\ref{sec:naive} in
floating point when that cancellation is nearly exact --- the same kind of
stress as \texttt{kinc2-driver/check\_gram\_minors.c} for the $\pi^0$ Gram
minors, but for the $\mu$-pair squared amplitude.

\subsection{Stress configuration}
\label{sec:numerics-setup}

Take a fixed mildly virtual two-photon kinematics ($W=20\,\mathrm{GeV}$,
$m_\mu=0.105658\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ in
$q_z=\sqrt{E^2+\varepsilon}$) and set
\begin{equation}
  e_1 = q_1 + \delta\,\hat x,\qquad e_2 = \hat y.
\end{equation}
At $\delta=0$ one has $e_1=q_1$, so the squared Ward identity of
Sections~\ref{sec:naive-checks} and~\ref{sec:form-checked} forces
$S=0$ exactly. For small $\delta$, $S=O(\delta^2)$ (or smaller) while
individual monomials in the expanded poly remain $O(1)$--$O(W^2)$: the
sum is a cancellation between large terms.

The poly is the committed header
\texttt{numeric/eval\_naive\_poly.h}, emitted from
\texttt{scripts/emit\_naive\_poly\_c.py} (same sympy sum as
Section~\ref{sec:naive}). The driver
\texttt{numeric/digit\_loss\_naive.c} evaluates it in \texttt{double} and
\texttt{long double}, and the same kinematics through the C~(3.5)
evaluator (\texttt{eval\_stable\_35.h}).

\subsection{What this section does and does not claim}
\label{sec:numerics-claims}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Claimed here:} evaluating the \emph{naive} 98-term poly in
    IEEE double near $e_1=q_1$ loses essentially all significant digits
    relative to the same poly in long double; the loss tracks the approach
    to the Ward zero.
  \item \textbf{Also claimed here:} side-by-side IEEE comparison of the
    naive poly against the C~(3.5) evaluator on this $\delta$-ray
    (\texttt{make -C numeric run}): as $\delta\to0$ the naive double value
    saturates near $10^{-13}$ while~(3.5) continues toward zero.
  \item \textbf{Not claimed here:} (i)~that relative double/long-double
    digits of~(3.5) itself stay high all the way to $\delta=0$ (both
    forms evaluate a quantity $\to0$, so relative digits dip; the
    absolute-value story is the clean claim); (ii)~a Monte Carlo fraction
    of phase-space points that are ``bad'' (Section~\ref{sec:mc});
    (iii)~that the near-gauge ray $e_1=q_1+\delta\hat x$ is identical to
    the physical $t_1,t_2\to0$ multiperipheral peak --- it is the cleanest
    probe of the same cancellation, not a full phase-space survey.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Monte Carlo scan}
\label{sec:mc}

Section~\ref{sec:numerics} showed digit loss along one near-gauge ray.

orientations, what \emph{fraction} of points is bad, and as a function of
what proximity?

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:mc-setup}

Driver: \texttt{numeric/mc\_digit\_loss.c}. Same poly header as

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Vermaseren's warning is concrete for
$\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$. Expanding $|{\cal M}|^2$ into 4-vector
products yields a 98-term polynomial
(Section~\ref{sec:naive}) whose Ward identity holds in both Python and
FORM (Sections~\ref{sec:naive-checks},~\ref{sec:symbolic-check}). The
stable Appendix-C /~(III.10) route hides that cancellation in
$\varepsilon$-tensor structure (Section~\ref{sec:stable}). Numerically,
IEEE double evaluation of the naive poly loses essentially all digits
near $e_1=q_1$ (Section~\ref{sec:numerics}), and the Monte Carlo fraction
of bad points tracks proximity to that Ward surface rather than photon
virtuality alone (Section~\ref{sec:mc}).

The $\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$ layer is closed
(naive$\equiv$(3.5), Section~\ref{sec:naive-eq-35}), the full
electron-dressed~(III.11) sum $\sum_{ij}M_{ij}\equiv L\otimes L$ is
checked corner-by-corner (Section~\ref{sec:M11-check}), and C evaluators
of~(3.5) and $\sum M_{ij}/B^2$ are wired into the digit-loss / MC drivers
(\texttt{numeric/eval\_stable\_35.h}, \texttt{numeric/eval\_mij.h}).
FORM Appendix~C reductions~(C.4)/(C.8)/(C.9)/(C.13)/(C.14) are gated by
\texttt{make -C form appC}. A genuine $2\to4$ multiperipheral kinematics
gate (\texttt{gamgam}/\texttt{peripp} vs $\sum M_{ij}$ on soft $|t|$
samples) is \texttt{make -C kinc2-driver check-eemm}; digit-loss of
$\sum M_{ij}/B^2$ vs L$\otimes$L(naive) on the same gamgam samples is
\texttt{make -C kinc2-driver digit-loss-eemm}. FORM transcription of the
$M_{ij}$ Levi-Civita forms vs their closed polynomials is
\texttt{make -C form mij}.

\subsection{Why the main text stops at one $T_{ij}$}

Hand-reducing all sixteen $T_{ij}$ through~\eqref{eq:contraction} is
possible but error-prone: the longest chains (e.g.\ $T_{11}$ and $T_{33}$)
are six-gamma traces with three non-commuting momenta from the
propagators, and the mixed $D_1 D_2$ blocks ($T_{13}$, $T_{31}$, \ldots)
couple $k_1$ and $k_2$. The trustworthy record of that algebra is the
program in \texttt{mumu\_trace\_lib.py}, not a second hand transcription.
What the appendix adds is (i)~the issues that actually broke checks during
development, (ii)~the per-$T_{ij}$ monomial counts, and (iii)~the raw
generated dump.

\subsection{Bugs and failed checks (kept visible)}

\begin{description}
  \item[Duplicated token in the amplitude Ward script.]
    An early version of \texttt{mumu\_gauge\_check.py} encoded diagram~1's
    $k$-piece as the word \texttt{['p6','k1','k1','e2']} instead of
    \texttt{['p6','k1','e2']}. The machine then disagreed with the hand
    reduction in that script's docstring. The hand reduction was right;
    the word list was wrong. After the fix, machine and hand agree:
    diagram~1 $\to-\slashed e_2$, diagram~2 $\to+\slashed e_2$.
  \item[Missing on-shell conditions in the squared Ward script.]
    With $p_6{\cdot}p_6$ and $p_7{\cdot}p_7$ left free,

    substitution was then applied in the naive sum. Re-running the naive
    script with the substitution present did not change the 98-term count
    relative to the earlier draft that omitted it in that script alone
    --- but the squared Ward check is the reason the substitution is
    mandatory for the pipeline as a whole.
  \item[Wrong Ward test on $|M|^2$.]
    Substituting $e_1\to q_1$ into \emph{both} $N$ and $\bar N$ makes the
    squared trace vanish for a trivial reason. That test was rejected; the
    script substitutes only on $N$.

\section{Verification of (3.5) and $M_{ij}$}
\label{app:verify}

Main-text Sections~\ref{sec:naive-eq-35} and~\ref{sec:M11-check} state the

\subsection{Two different $Q$'s in the paper}
\label{app:Q-convention}

Vermaseren uses the letter $Q$ in two incompatible ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item In~(3.5)/(3.6) (subreaction $\gamma^*\gamma^*\to\mu^+\mu^-$),
    $Q\equiv p_6-p_7$. Numerically,
    $D_1 D_2=(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$ holds with that $Q$, and
    $D_1=-q_1{\cdot}q_2-Q{\cdot}q_1$, $D_2=-q_1{\cdot}q_2+Q{\cdot}q_1$
    reproduce $k_1^2-m^2$ and $k_2^2-m^2$.
  \item In~(3.10)/(III.10) the same letter appears in the denominator
    factor $(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$ with the structural reading
    $Q=q_1+q_2$ used in Topic~6's denominator note. That identity is a
    different algebraic object (asymmetric in $t_1$).
\end{itemize}
All checks in this appendix use $Q=p_6-p_7$, matching~(3.5) and the
$M_{ij}$ blocks as transcribed from Nucl.\ Phys.\ B229~(1983)~355--356.

\subsection{Transcribed (3.5) used by the checker}
\label{app:eq35-formula}

With
$\mathrm{Tr}[i{\cdot}j]=\varepsilon^{q_i e_i\mu\nu}\varepsilon_{q_j e_j\nu\mu}$,
$Q{\cdot}1{\cdot}2{\cdot}Q=\varepsilon^{q_1 e_1 Q\mu}\varepsilon_{q_2 e_2\mu Q}$,
and $B=(q_1{\cdot}q_2)^2-(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2$, the implementation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  |{\cal M}|^2_{(3.5)}
  &= B^{-2}\Big\{
      8B\big(\tfrac14(\mathrm{Tr}[1{\cdot}2])^2
            -\varepsilon^{q_1 e_1 q_2 e_2}\varepsilon_{q_1 e_1 q_2 e_2}\big) \\
  &\quad
      -2(Q{\cdot}q_1)^2\,\mathrm{Tr}[1{\cdot}1]\,\mathrm{Tr}[2{\cdot}2] \\
  &\quad
      -8\big(Q{\cdot}1{\cdot}2{\cdot}Q
            -\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}[1{\cdot}2](Q^2+q_1{\cdot}q_2)\big)^2 \\
  &\quad
      -\mathrm{Tr}[1{\cdot}1]
        \big(\varepsilon^{q_2 e_2\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_{q_1 Q\alpha\beta}\big)^2 \\
  &\quad
      -\mathrm{Tr}[2{\cdot}2]
        \big(\varepsilon^{q_1 e_1\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_{q_2 Q\alpha\beta}\big)^2
    \Big\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:35-implemented}
\end{equation}
Minkowski $(+---)$ reductions used in code (cross-checked against explicit
index sums):
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{Tr}[i{\cdot}j]
    &= 2\big((q_i{\cdot}q_j)(e_i{\cdot}e_j)-(q_i{\cdot}e_j)(e_i{\cdot}q_j)\big),
    \label{eq:Tr-id} \\
  \varepsilon^{abcd}\varepsilon_{abcd}
    &= \det\!\big(G(a,b,c,d)\big),
    \quad G_{ij}=v_i{\cdot}v_j,
    \label{eq:eps4-id} \\
  \varepsilon^{ab\mu\nu}\varepsilon_{cd\mu\nu}
    &= -2\big((a{\cdot}c)(b{\cdot}d)-(a{\cdot}d)(b{\cdot}c)\big).
    \label{eq:biline-id}
\end{align}
The chain $Q{\cdot}1{\cdot}2{\cdot}Q$ is the $3\times3$ Gram minor of
$(q_1,e_1,Q)$ against $(q_2,e_2,Q)$.

\subsection{Scanned-sign correction on the last two bilinears}
\label{app:sign-fix}

A literal reading of the scanned~(3.5) suggests overall \emph{plus} signs
in front of the two $\mathrm{Tr}[i{\cdot}i](\varepsilon{\cdot}\varepsilon)^2$
terms. Least-squares fitting of those six schematic coefficients against
the naive poly on random samples returned
\[
  (2,\,-8,\,-2,\,-8,\,-1,\,-1)
\]
for
$(\mathrm{Tr}_{12}^2,\,\varepsilon^4,\,\mathrm{Tr}_{11}\mathrm{Tr}_{22},\,
(\cdots)^2,\,\mathrm{Tr}_{11}\varepsilon_2^2,\,\mathrm{Tr}_{22}\varepsilon_1^2)$,
i.e.\ the printed $\tfrac14$ and the $-2$, $-8$ coefficients match, but
the last two bilinears must be \emph{minus}. Equation~\eqref{eq:35-implemented}
uses that correction. Without it, relative errors sit at the
$10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ level; with it,
\texttt{mumu\_stable\_35\_check.py} reports
\texttt{PASS} (100 samples: 50 physical-transverse, 50 near-gauge;
relative error $<10^{-8}$, or absolute $<10^{-11}$ near $S=0$).

\subsection{$M_{ij}/B^2\equiv W_i\otimes W_j$ method}
\label{app:M11-method}

The point-particle tensor~(3.8) splits into
$L_{W_1}=2q^2(g-qq/q^2)$ and $L_{W_2}=8\,r_\mu r_\nu$,
$r=p-(p{\cdot}q)/q^2\,q$. If
$S(e_1,e_2)=H_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}e_1^\mu e_1^\nu e_2^\rho e_2^\sigma$
(schematically), the script recovers the quadratic form in each
polarization by finite differences on the coordinate basis,
\[
  H_{\mu\nu}
  =\begin{cases}
     S(e_{(\mu)}) & \mu=\nu,\\
     \tfrac12\big(S(e_{(\mu)}+e_{(\nu)})-S(e_{(\mu)})-S(e_{(\nu)})\big)
       & \mu\neq\nu,
   \end{cases}
\]
then contracts $H_{\mu\nu}L^{W_i\,\mu\nu}$. Nested in $e_1$ then $e_2$,
that yields $S_{W_i W_j}$. The falsifiable identities are
$M_{ij}/B^2=S_{W_i W_j}$ for each corner and
$\sum_{ij}M_{ij}/B^2=S_{LL}$ (not $M_{ij}/(q_1^2 q_2^2 B)^2$: the
$(q_1^2 q_2^2)^2$ of~(3.10) is already carried by the $2q^2$ factors inside
each $L_{W_1}$). Samples use massless $p_i$ with $2p_i{\cdot}q_i=q_i^2$.

\subsubsection*{Transcription notes (NPB~355--356)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $M_{11}$: Levi-Civita-free; transcribed as~\eqref{eq:M11}
    (prefactor $64\,q_1^2 q_2^2$).
  \item $M_{12}$/$M_{21}$: NPB writes
    $B(-\varepsilon^{p_2 q_2 q_1\mu}\varepsilon_{p_2 q_2 q_1\mu}-\cdots)$.
    The Gram helper $\texttt{eps3sq}=-\det G(a,b,c)$ used for three-index
    contractions has the opposite overall sign relative to that
    typesetting, so the implementation uses
    $B\bigl(\texttt{eps3sq}(p_2,q_2,q1)+\texttt{eps3sq}(p_2,q_2,Q)\bigr)$
    (and the $M_{21}$ swap). The mix bilinears are
    $\varepsilon^{p_2 q_2\mu\nu}\varepsilon_{q_1 Q\mu\nu}$ as printed.
  \item $M_{22}$: overall $512$ times the brace on p.~356. The
    $\tfrac18\mathrm{Tr}_{11}(\varepsilon^{p_2 q_2}\varepsilon_{q_1 Q})^2$
    terms contract the \emph{second} pair against $(q_1,Q)$ or $(q_2,Q)$,
    not $(p_1,Q)$ --- a naive $(q_i,e_i)\to(p_i,q_i)$ map of~(3.5) that
    also remaps those slots fails at the $10^{-3}$ level.
\end{itemize}

PASS gate: relative error $<10^{-5}$ ($M_{11}$) / $<10^{-4}$ (other
corners and full sum) on 30 random multiperipheral samples. Observed
worst relative errors:
$M_{11}\sim2\times10^{-7}$,
$M_{12}/M_{21}\sim5\times10^{-7}$,
$M_{22}\sim2\times10^{-5}$,
full $\sim2\times10^{-5}$ (finite-difference Hessian).
